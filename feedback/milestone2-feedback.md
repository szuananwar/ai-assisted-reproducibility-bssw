## Review Priorities

Reviewers are asked to focus on:

- technical and scientific accuracy;
- clarity and usefulness of the best-practice recommendations;
- appropriateness for scientific software, AI, and HPC audiences;
- distinction between reproducibility readiness and successful reproduction;
- treatment of AI as grounded assistance rather than an independent scientific judge;
- quality and practicality of examples and exercises;
- missing domain-specific considerations;
- accessibility for students, researchers, and research software engineers; and
- places where claims should be narrowed, clarified, or supported with stronger evidence.

### Status definitions

- **Prepared:** the material is complete enough to be reviewed or tested.
- **Shared:** the material has actually been sent or made available to an identified reviewer/tester with a request for feedback.
- **Feedback Received:** substantive comments, observations, or testing results have been received and documented.
- **Revision Incorporated:** actionable feedback has been addressed through a documented revision, or a reasoned decision not to change has been recorded.

## Feedback Integration Principles

Feedback will be evaluated against the fellowship's core principles:

1. **Evidence before automation.** Recommendations should be grounded in observable artifacts or documented workflow information.
2. **Human oversight.** AI-generated guidance should remain subject to researcher or expert review.
3. **Readiness is not proof of reproduction.** Repository evidence and rubric scores must not be presented as certification of scientific reproducibility.
4. **Applicability matters.** Not every scientific software project requires the same artifacts or infrastructure.
5. **Quality matters as well as presence.** The usefulness and completeness of an artifact can be more important than whether a file merely exists.
